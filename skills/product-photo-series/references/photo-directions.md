# Product Photo Directions

Read sections relevant to material, space or operation difficulties. Specialized drinkware guidance is under "Drinkware structure and state."

## How material changes lighting

Select from visible surfaces or reliable information, not category assumptions. Light should make material and key information readable. These methods do not guarantee exact optical simulation by a model.

- **Transparent glass and liquid:** Use background contrast for edge separation and broad reflections or side/back light for curves. Inspect opening, base, liquid level and label as needed. Preserve transparency and color.
- **Shiny metal or coating:** Shape continuous light/dark reflections to describe curvature without glare covering the outline. Use side light to emphasize the direction of brushed surfaces; organize mirror surfaces with broad light/dark reflection shapes.
- **Matte packaging and plastic:** Soft directional light reveals volume and controls hotspots. Keep labels readable and preserve the actual surface.
- **Fabric and soft materials:** Side light can show weave, seams and folds. Shape, thickness and support follow the reference.
- **Complex package print:** Orient the recognizable face toward the viewer, avoiding strong reflections across text. Preserve patterns and colors with the product.

One product can have several surfaces. Choose the image's focus and light the relevant part rather than requiring one sentence for every material.

## Product, people, props and space

Derive what happens, what touches the product and where it sits from its purpose. Express warmth or restraint through particular light, color and placement.

- **Support and scale:** Align base, surface, contact shadow and occlusion. Do not invent centimeters. People or familiar objects can provide scale clues without changing product proportions.
- **Unoccupied setting:** Placement and context indicate preparation, storage or use. Supporting objects provide use or scale clues while keeping the recognizable face visible.
- **Hands or partial person:** Specify contact and action, such as holding a cup, wearing a ring or pressing a visible button. Keep hand/product relationships readable. "Natural use" alone may encourage invented mechanisms.
- **Model or identity:** People explain wearing, scale or brand expression. A specified identity needs corresponding material; a style reference's person is not automatically the subject.
- **Operation and effects:** Establish structure/function before describing action. A closed package does not imply an opening, pouring or glowing demonstration. Performance needs user-provided support.

## Multiple-reference example

Example: image 1 supplies product form and front print; image 2 supplies only the product-right/text-space-left relationship, not its brand or bottle; image 3 supplies soft side light. These are not three products that must appear together.

When style conflicts with readability, prioritize the user's stated goal and explain the tradeoff. Style references cannot establish real structure. For a requested view lacking materials, explain the needed references or the alternative view and what changes.

## Drinkware structure and state

Read only for cups, kettles, thermoses or similar drinkware when opening state or use actions need a decision.

- Distinguish body, rim, lid, handle, drinking opening and seal connections. Open-lid shots need evidence of the opening and inner lid. With only a closed reference, explain the additional reference needed or an alternative using the closed state.
- State opening state and lid/body positions. Pouring, drinking or cleaning follow a confirmed mechanism; preparation, contact and component positions must be compatible.
- A receiving cup explains pouring; a hand explains grip or opening. Arrange them for this task's purpose. Steam or splashes must correspond to the actual liquid, opening and action; a closed body cannot emit steam merely for atmosphere.
- Inspect the opening, inner lid, connections and hand contact. Transparent parts also need readable edges, thickness and layering.

## Local-edit example

Target an observed deviation, for example: "Make the latch and inner lid readable together, preserving appearance, colors, print and composition." Inspect retained features after editing; unchanged pixels are not guaranteed.
