# Product Photo Directions

Read sections relevant to the purpose and difficulty. These are overridable creative starting points, not required fields or validated recipes for every category.

## How purpose changes layout

| Purpose | Priority | View, distance and layout |
|---|---|---|
| Overall appearance | Recognizable form, colors and labels | Show the full outline and key face; separate edges from background. Avoid exaggerated perspective that changes shape. Follow platform rules supplied by the user. |
| Website banner / advertising | Product and copy work together | Establish text placement before product location, scale and viewing direction. Negative space must be usable for typography. Preserve product labels. |
| Detail / explanation | Readable component or mechanism | Include the part and necessary adjacent structure. Choose angle and depth of field for all required components; do not automatically apply shallow-focus macro. |
| Use / wearing | Scale, action or environment | Define one plausible interaction and the role of person, product and setting. Keep important parts visible despite hands, clothing or background. |
| Coordinated set | Different information about one product | Preserve appearance and agreed style; change purpose, viewing distance or contact relationship rather than merely renaming backgrounds. |

Studio means a controlled setting; people are an expression choice. They are compatible. Banners need not exclude people, and white backgrounds do not imply deleting branding.

## How material changes lighting

Select from visible surfaces or reliable information, not category assumptions. Light should make material and key information readable. These methods do not guarantee exact optical simulation by a model.

- **Transparent glass and liquid:** Use background contrast for edge separation and broad reflections or side/back light for curves. Inspect opening, base, liquid level and label as needed. Preserve transparency and color; do not invent bubbles, condensation or internal scenery.
- **Shiny metal or coating:** Shape continuous light/dark reflections to describe curvature rather than obscuring the outline with white glare. Do not describe all metal as brushed or replace the surface for a "premium" look.
- **Matte packaging and plastic:** Soft directional light reveals volume and controls hotspots; preserve label legibility. Plastic is a valid material, not something to erase with a universal prohibition.
- **Fabric and soft materials:** Side light can show weave, seams and folds. Shape, thickness and support follow the reference. A clean presentation does not automatically need wrinkles or "real imperfections."
- **Complex package print:** Orient the recognizable face toward the viewer, avoiding strong reflections across text. Preserve patterns and colors with the product. Original labels and additional advertising text are separate choices.

One product can have several surfaces. Choose the image's focus and light the relevant part rather than requiring one sentence for every material.

## Product, people, props and space

Derive what happens, what touches the product and where it sits from its purpose. Warmth or restraint comes from particular light, color and placement, not a compulsory window, bathroom, marble or hand.

- **Support and scale:** Align base, surface, contact shadow and occlusion. Do not invent centimeters. People or familiar objects can provide scale clues without changing product proportions.
- **Unoccupied setting:** Placement and context indicate preparation, storage or use. Hands and a fixed prop count are unnecessary. Supporting objects need a role and must not obscure the recognizable face.
- **Hands or partial person:** Specify contact and action, such as holding a cup, wearing a ring or pressing a visible button. Keep hand/product relationships readable. "Natural use" alone may encourage invented mechanisms.
- **Model or identity:** People explain wearing, scale or brand expression. A specified identity needs corresponding material; a style reference's person is not automatically the subject.
- **Operation and effects:** Establish structure/function before describing action. A closed package does not imply an opening, pouring or glowing demonstration. Performance needs user-provided support.

## Multiple references

Product, layout, style and identity may come from different inputs. Name inputs before describing their scope in the prompt.

Example: image 1 supplies product form and front print; image 2 supplies only the product-right/text-space-left relationship, not its brand or bottle; image 3 supplies soft side light. These are not three products that must appear together.

When style conflicts with readability, prioritize the user's stated goal and explain the tradeoff. Request material or choose a known view for unseen backs, interiors or connections. Style references cannot establish real structure.

## From plan to prompt

State changes and retained features with concrete terms. Add light, lens, material and background only when useful for composition, appearance or readability. Consult [worked examples](product-photo-examples.md) when fuller reasoning helps; do not copy them into every task.

Target local edits at an observed deviation, for example: "Make the latch and inner lid readable together, preserving appearance, colors, print and composition." Inspect the result; unchanged pixels are not guaranteed.
