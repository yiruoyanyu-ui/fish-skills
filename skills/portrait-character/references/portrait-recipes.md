# Portrait Directions

Read this guide when the portrait brief is vague, a character needs recognizable design choices, or the same person must continue across images. It provides decision dimensions rather than a fixed style catalog. Explicit user choices take priority.

## Start from the image's purpose

Decide what the viewer needs to understand about the person and what role the person has in the image. An avatar prioritizes facial recognition and expression at display size. A character portrait prioritizes role and styling. A contextual portrait also needs a readable action, relationship or environmental cue. Purpose informs framing, pose and information priority; it does not prescribe a face.

Translate terms such as "attractive," "natural" or "premium" into observable choices: a coherent silhouette and styling, an expression suited to the moment, a readable subject, lighting that supports face and wardrobe, and a treatment suited to the intended use. When no further direction exists, choose one coherent direction rather than combining competing aesthetic templates.

## Create recognition with a few traits

Choose a small, relevant set of traits from dimensions such as:

- facial shape and feature relationships;
- hairstyle, hair color and hairline silhouette;
- build, posture and characteristic movement;
- wardrobe silhouette, materials and color relationships;
- expression, gaze and manner of engagement;
- accessories or details meaningful to the character.

The traits should reinforce one another rather than fill every category. Different characters need readable distinctions, but those distinctions should not depend on exaggerated ethnicity, age, body type or gender. Convey labels such as villain, executive or approachable through performance, styling and context instead of translating them into a fixed face, skin tone or body.

## Separate continuity from the current state

| Stable identity | Current image state |
|---|---|
| Facial relationships, hair shape or color, build, signature details | Expression, gaze, action, wardrobe variation, setting, composition, lighting |

Create a character record only when the person will be reused across images. Record the character's name or purpose, stable traits, allowed variations, the selected appearance reference and styling that must continue. Keep user-provided facts and visible traits separate from creative proposals.

## Respect realism, retouching and stylization

- **Natural realism:** coordinate expression, gaze, pose, skin, hair, wardrobe and environmental light. Natural does not automatically require wrinkles, freckles, visible pores, grain, messiness or asymmetry.
- **Fashion retouching:** polished skin, smooth makeup, dramatic lighting and exaggerated styling can be intentional. Correct unintended structural, material or lighting errors without treating retouching itself as a failure.
- **Illustration or surreal work:** follow the selected medium's shape language, line, color and exaggeration. Do not add photographic skin detail, lens language or noise by default.

The user's chosen medium takes priority. Visual terminology should support the person, pose, setting and composition rather than replace character design with a list of camera parameters.

## Compose the instruction

Use only the sections relevant to the task:

```text
identity and stable traits
→ expression, gaze, posture or action in this moment
→ wardrobe, hair, makeup and meaningful details
→ setting, framing, viewpoint and compositional relationships
→ lighting or visual medium that supports the portrayal
→ traits to retain, change or avoid
```

Resolve what the person should communicate before choosing photographic or illustrative techniques. The final instruction should be coherent, free of contradictions and leave the generation model reasonable freedom to compose the image.
