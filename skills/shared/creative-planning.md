# Creative Planning · Limited Candidate

Read as needed for product photos, covers or UGC tasks requiring concept comparison, reference analysis or multiple shots. Execute simple images and already-defined plans directly. This method does not require a new tool, extra generation, fixed table or disclosure of every reasoning step.

## Turn a brief into an executable plan

Separate user requirements and source facts, creative choices for this task, and defaults filling gaps. Defaults are overridable; shared guidance does not fix ratio, shot count, colors, lettering or materials. Ask only questions that change the deliverable and cannot reasonably be resolved.

For one image, identify the subject and main visual relationship, then choose composition, light and setting to express them. State whether references supply layout, style, action or identity. Background, labels and model guesses are not product/person facts. Alternatives must differ meaningfully; do not manufacture options for a one-image request.

## Video: give each shot a purpose

For each necessary shot, identify what it communicates, its action, relationship to adjacent shots and sound. Count and length serve the expression. One action may work as a long take; comparison, process or results may require multiple shots.

Align narration with what the scene can support. Plan expression and rhythm before checking actual generated shot boundaries and sound durations. A planned timetable is not proof that a model executed it.

## Write the prompt

Clearly describe necessary subjects, relationships, actions, retained features and this task's style. Remove repeated adjectives, conflicting instructions and unrelated bans. The final prompt need not be longer; retain decisions that affect the result.

Keep creative intent separate from interface configuration. Discover reference roles, model, resolution, duration, audio capability and prices from current Fish tools. Accepting references does not guarantee exact detail or cut-point reproduction.

After generation, compare with the original brief and sources. Record whether a deviation appears to involve the plan, prompt, model execution or processing; keep uncertain causes as hypotheses. Keep case feedback scoped to that case. Promoting a default requires scope, counterexamples and independent acceptance.

This module adapts responsibility separation and planning concepts, not Flova prompts or its runtime. Benefits across Skills remain unproven.
